How Long Does Food Last in the Fridge? The Complete Shelf-Life Guide

If you have ever stood in front of an open fridge, sniffed a carton of milk and thought "is this still good?", you are not alone. Roughly one-third of all food produced for people never gets eaten, and a huge share of that waste starts at home — in the fridge. The good news: most food waste is preventable with one simple habit: knowing how long does food last in fridge conditions, and acting on that knowledge before the clock runs out.

This guide is your complete food shelf life chart walkthrough. It explains why different foods expire at different speeds, how storage choices (fridge vs. freezer vs. pantry, opened vs. unopened) change the math, what your fridge food expiration guide should account for, and how a simple tracking habit can save a family hundreds of dollars a year. KeepFresh, the free tool at the top of this page, turns all of this into a practical daily habit: add what you bought, and it computes the exact use-by date for you.

Why a Food Shelf Life Chart Matters More Than the Date on the Package

Those printed dates — "best by", "sell by", "use by" — are the manufacturer's best guess about quality, not a lab-tested safety deadline. In the United States, for example, only infant formula is federally required to carry a true expiration date. Everything else is voluntary, inconsistent, and often conservative. That means two identical cartons of yogurt can behave very differently depending on how they were stored after you brought them home.

A reliable food shelf life chart starts from storage reality, not the label. Public guidance from agencies like the USDA's FoodKeeper program estimates shelf life by food type and storage zone: fridge, freezer, or pantry. KeepFresh's built-in database of 170+ foods is compiled from that public guidance and marked as estimates at build time — because temperature, packaging, and handling vary, but the patterns are remarkably consistent.

The three factors that control shelf life

  • Temperature. Bacteria multiply fastest between 40°F and 140°F (4°C–60°C) — the "danger zone". A fridge at or below 40°F (4°C) slows them dramatically; a freezer at 0°F (-18°C) stops them almost entirely.
  • Moisture and air. Fresh produce breathes and wilts; exposed meat oxidizes. Airtight wrapping can add days of life.
  • Acidity and salt. Fermented, pickled and cured foods (yogurt, pickles, salami) last far longer than fresh milk or raw chicken — acidity and salt are hostile to spoilage microbes.

Fridge Food Expiration Guide: What Lasts, What Doesn't

Here is the core of any fridge food expiration guide: perishables fall into a few clear tiers. Raw ground meat, fresh fish, and delicate seafood have the shortest fridge lives — about 1–2 days. Raw chicken pieces, pork, and sausages last roughly 1–2 days too, while whole cuts of beef, pork roasts and steaks buy you 3–5 days. Cooked leftovers — casseroles, soups, cooked meat, leftover pizza — hold for 3–4 days. Opened deli meats and hot dogs are good for 3–7 days depending on the product.

Dairy is more forgiving. Unopened milk lasts about a week past its sell-by date in a cold fridge; hard cheeses like cheddar and parmesan keep for months; soft cheeses and yogurt last 1–2 weeks. Eggs, kept in their carton in the main body of the fridge (not the door), stay good for 3–5 weeks — one of the most misunderstood items in the kitchen.

Fresh produce varies wildly: berries and leafy greens fade in 2–5 days, while carrots, cabbage and apples keep for weeks. The tracker above handles all of this automatically — search any of the 170 foods and it applies the right estimate for your chosen storage.

Opened vs. Unopened: The Detail Most Guides Skip

One of the most common questions behind "how long does food last in fridge" searches is really about opened packages. An unopened carton of chicken broth can sit in the pantry for a year; open it and you get 3–4 days in the fridge. Unopened yogurt keeps a week or more past its date; opened, you have days. Ketchup, mustard and pickles survive months once opened because acidity protects them — but mayonnaise, salsa and opened pasta sauce do not get that luxury.

Fridge and Freezer Temperature: The Invisible Variable

Every shelf-life estimate assumes your fridge is actually cold. Food-safety guidance is unanimous: the fridge must stay at or below 40°F (4°C), and the freezer at 0°F (-18°C). Above 40°F, bacteria double as fast as every 20 minutes. A fridge running at 45°F can cut the safe life of milk and meat nearly in half — silently.

Freezer Strategy: Your Second Fridge

The freezer is the great pause button. Most meats keep 3–12 months frozen; bread 3 months; blanched vegetables 8–12 months; berries up to a year. The enemy is freezer burn (dehydration), not bacteria — food kept at 0°F stays safe indefinitely, though quality fades. Wrap tightly, remove air, label with the freeze date, and rotate: first in, first out. Common Mistakes That Shorten Shelf Life

  • Storing milk and eggs in the door. The door is the warmest spot. Keep dairy on middle shelves.
  • Washing berries before storing. Moisture invites mold — wash right before eating.
  • Putting hot leftovers straight in the fridge. Cool within 2 hours; divide large pots into shallow containers so they chill fast.
  • Trusting the sniff test alone. Spoilage bacteria usually announce themselves, but pathogens often don't. Dates + senses + proper temperature is the safe trio.
  • Forgetting the freezer exists. Bread, butter, shredded cheese, bananas for smoothies — all freeze beautifully.

Frequently asked questions

How long does food last in the fridge?

It depends on the food. Raw ground meat and fresh fish last 1–2 days; raw chicken pieces 1–2 days; beef steaks and roasts 3–5 days; cooked leftovers 3–4 days; opened milk about a week; hard cheeses months; eggs 3–5 weeks. These assume your fridge is at or below 40°F (4°C) — warmer fridges shorten every number. KeepFresh computes the exact use-by date for 170+ foods from your bought-on date.

Does opening a package shorten how long food lasts?

Almost always, yes. Opening exposes food to air, moisture and bacteria. Unopened broth lasts a year in the pantry but 3–4 days in the fridge once opened; unopened yogurt keeps well past its date while opened yogurt lasts days. Use KeepFresh's "Already opened" toggle and it automatically applies the shorter opened shelf life.

What temperature should my fridge and freezer be?

Your fridge should be at or below 40°F (4°C), ideally 32–40°F (0–4°C). Your freezer should be at 0°F (-18°C) or colder. Above 40°F, bacteria can double every 20 minutes, which is why the temp checker in this tool flags anything warmer. An inexpensive appliance thermometer is the easiest way to verify.

Can I eat food after its use-by date?

"Best by" and "sell by" dates are quality estimates, not safety deadlines — most foods are fine past them if stored correctly and they look, smell and taste normal. True "use by" dates on highly perishable items (like infant formula or some deli meats) should be respected. When in doubt, throw it out — and note that pathogens don't always smell, so proper temperature matters more than sniffing.

How long does food last in the freezer?

At 0°F (-18°C), food stays safe indefinitely — quality is what fades. Practical freezer lives: ground meat 3–4 months, steaks and chicken 6–12 months, bread 3 months, blanched vegetables 8–12 months, berries up to a year. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn, label the freeze date, and rotate first-in-first-out.

How can tracking expiry dates save me money?

Households waste hundreds of dollars of food yearly — mostly from forgetting what's in the fridge.
